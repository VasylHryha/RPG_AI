# C6 redesign: options after the design-gate stop (SUPERSEDED, see experiments/c6_proposal_r2.md)

Status: **PROPOSED (options)**, awaiting the owner's choice. Nothing will run, and no code changes, until the owner
chooses and approves. Context: decision 0009 (`docs/decisions/0009-c6-stops-at-design-gate.md`) and the gate record
`evidence/c6_design_gate/SUMMARY.md`. Authority: RRG v0.2 locked core, the R4 standard, and decision 0007.

## 1. What failed, in one paragraph

Level-2 groups lock into level-3 assemblies, and they stay distinct from one another. But contact with neighbouring
groups compresses the C4 units **inside** each group until they interpenetrate. Alone, the same groups stay
healthy: 1 of 606 units overlap against 167 of 606 in contact. So in this model, building level 3 crushes level 1.

## 2. Why, from the physics (reasoning, not yet measured)

The C4 element law has **one interaction channel**, a short-range one. It does three things:
- it attracts in-phase neighbours, A(1 + J cos Δθ);
- it repels through B/r;
- it acts only among each element's 8 nearest neighbours within radius 3.

Two consequences follow:
1. **Coupling between groups needs contact.** A boundary element of a 40-element group finds its 8 nearest
   neighbours inside its own group, so separated groups do not interact. The pilot and the gate both showed this.
   The only way to couple level-2 groups is to press them together.
2. **There is no separation of binding scales.** The same attraction that holds elements inside a unit also pulls
   across units and across groups. Nothing makes the bond between groups weaker than the bonds inside them, so the
   level-3 bond deforms the inside. The B/r repulsion is soft enough that, under that pull, units can interpenetrate.

There is a candidate *downward* contribution as well: locking into level 3 may shift the phase offsets between units
inside a group toward in-phase, which raises their mutual attraction. That would be a whole-to-parts effect turning
destructive. It is unmeasured; option 0 below can separate it from plain mechanical pressure.

**What the theory says about this.** The locked core allows each scale its own *dominant interactions* (§7). The
framework's interaction-channel extension (doc 02 §19–20; doc 07) says a new level typically rests on a weaker or
different effective channel than the level below: molecules do not crush nuclei, because chemical binding is far
weaker than nuclear binding. The C4 law has no such separation, so recursion stalls at R₁. This is a property of the
model, not a contradiction of the locked core.

## 4. Rules any redesign must keep

- **Same procedure at every level** (decision 0007), with the normalization ledger derived from invariant 1.
- **No tuning toward the result.** For option 2, the parameter ladder (for example ε ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.05} × K and
  R_long ∈ {2, 3} × the median level-1 radius of gyration) and the selection rule are registered *before*
  development data.
  - Proposed rule: the smallest ε, then the smallest R_long, under which development level-1 **and** level-2
    formation stay at least at their registered targets.
  - Level-3 formation is **not** used for selection, so the selection cannot aim at the level-3 result.
- **Re-qualification:** under any changed law, the level-1 pool (the C4 detector) and transition 2 are re-measured
  on fresh worlds before any level-3 claim. C4 R003 and C5 R003 stay accepted for the original law.
- **The C++ engine:** a changed law needs a new NumPy reference in a new file (the frozen `c4_model` cannot change),
  an updated kernel, and the same four equivalence checks.
- **Honest scope:** a C6 result under option 2 is about "C4 law plus a weak long-range phase channel". It says
  nothing about whether such a channel *emerges* from lower-level organization.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Option 0 first.** It costs minutes, and it decides whether the problem is mechanical (pointing to option 1 or
   2) or a destructive downward phase effect (pointing to option 2, or to a finding worth recording in itself).
2. **Then option 2,** if the owner wants the recursion test to continue. It is the option best aligned with the
   theory's own account of how new levels form, provided its parameters are fixed by the pre-declared rule above.
3. **Option 3** is legitimate if the owner prefers to record the model limit and pause the hierarchy lane.

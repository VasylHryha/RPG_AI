# RRG v0.1 — Scientific Framework

## 1. Research status

**Recursive Resonant Geometry (RRG)** is a working unification hypothesis, not an established physical theory.

The framework asks whether a repeated relation found across many known systems can be promoted into a scale-recursive organizing principle:

\[
\boxed{
\text{relational geometry}
\leftrightarrow
\text{temporal mode structure}
}
\]

followed by:

\[
\boxed{
\text{persistent coupled structures}
\rightarrow
\text{new effective unit at a higher scale}
}
\]

The crucial research question is not whether individual pieces of this occur — many do — but whether **one mathematical rule remains approximately form-invariant under coarse-graining and can generate several successive levels without hand-authoring a different mechanism for each level**.

### Evidence labels used below

- **Established:** directly part of accepted or well-supported science.
- **Compatible:** known science demonstrates a mechanism of the same abstract form.
- **RRG hypothesis:** an extrapolation RRG must derive/test.
- **Open:** unresolved even in conventional science, or unresolved within RRG.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Relational geometry \(G\)

Geometry is used broadly:

\[
G=
\{\text{components, positions, connectivity, order, orientation, boundaries, constraints}\}
\]

It is not restricted to Euclidean visible shape.

### 2.2 Temporal mode structure \(M\)

RRG's earlier conversational use of “frequency” is formalized as:

\[
M=
\{\omega,\phi,A,\psi(x),\text{propagation},\text{coupling},\tau,\ldots\}
\]

where:

- \(\omega\) = characteristic frequency/frequencies,
- \(\phi\) = phase relations,
- \(A\) = amplitude/state strength,
- \(\psi(x)\) = spatial mode pattern,
- \(\tau\) = characteristic timescale,
- coupling = how modes exchange influence/energy.

A single scalar frequency is not sufficient to determine a physical geometry.

### 2.3 Resonant dynamic geometry \(R\)

\[
\boxed{R=(G,M)}
\]

A geometry and compatible mode structure considered together.

### 2.4 Stability

A geometry is persistent when its coupled dynamics remain within, or return toward, an organized set after small perturbations.

Conceptually:

\[
\boxed{
G\rightarrow M\rightarrow G
}
\]

For fixed points, periodic orbits, quasiperiodic states, and attractors, different mathematical stability tests may be appropriate.

### 2.5 Collapse / transformation

When perturbations drive the system outside the stable region of its current higher-level organization:

\[
R_A\rightarrow R_B
\]

or:

\[
R_A\rightarrow\{R_i\}_{\text{lower scale}}
\]

The energy and lower-level structures need not disappear when a higher-level organization does.

### 2.6 Scale

A level of description at which a lower-level persistent structure can be treated as one effective degree of freedom.

RRG does **not** assume equally spaced scales or one universal scale factor.

## 3. Core RRG propositions

### Proposition 1 — Geometry and mode are mutually constraining

\[
\boxed{
G\leftrightarrow M
}
\]

**Established/compatible examples:** normal modes of strings, cavities, molecules, lattices, planets; synchronization systems where spatial organization and phase dynamics interact.

**RRG extension:** treat this mutual constraint as the primitive language of physical organization across scales.

### Proposition 2 — Stable organization is dynamic closure

\[
\boxed{
R=(G,M)\text{ persists when }G\text{ supports }M\text{ and }M\text{ reproduces/maintains }G
}
\]

This does not require static equilibrium.

### Proposition 3 — Coupling creates new degrees of freedom

\[
\boxed{
\{R_n^i\}
\rightarrow
R_{n+1}
}
\]

The lower components remain internally active while some freedoms are constrained and new collective freedoms/modes emerge.

### Proposition 4 — Whole and parts form circular causality

\[
\boxed{
\text{parts create collective order}
\leftrightarrow
\text{collective order constrains parts}
}
\]

This abstract form is strongly compatible with Haken's Synergetics and its “slaving principle” / circular causality.

### Proposition 5 — Persistent higher structures become new effective units

Once a collective structure is stable enough, the next scale can treat it as a single unit:

\[
R_{n+1}=\mathcal C(\{R_n\})
\]

where \(\mathcal C\) is a coarse-graining/composition rule.

### Proposition 6 — Variation comes primarily from arrangement and mode

Different higher-level identities can arise from the same types of lower-level components:

\[
\text{same parts}+
\text{different }G,M
\rightarrow
\text{different effective object}
\]

Composition matters, but organization can be equally decisive.

### Proposition 7 — Stability compresses complexity

A persistent structure packages lower-level complexity into a reusable effective unit.

This is not information-theoretic compression in a fully defined technical sense yet; it is an RRG research interpretation to formalize later.

### Proposition 8 — New stable structures can open new possibility spaces

A stable structure can generate pressure, temperature, fields, gradients, boundaries, or catalytic environments that allow additional configurations to become persistent.

Examples include stars enabling nucleosynthesis and biological systems maintaining chemical environments far from equilibrium.

## 4. What established science already supports

### 4.1 Coupled resonators create collective modes — Established

Coupled resonators can generate bonding, antibonding, Bloch, and other supermodes belonging to the collective system rather than any isolated component.

A recent rigorous treatment of open coupled resonators explicitly derives mode hybridization and bonding/antibonding supermodes.

### 4.2 Spatial organization and synchronization can co-evolve — Established

Swarmalator models couple spatial motion with internal phase dynamics.

A 2025 experimental realization showed synchronization and spatial dynamics co-evolving into synchronized clusters, rotating aggregates, and dispersive states.

This is a direct example of:

\[
G\rightarrow M
\quad\text{and}\quad
M\rightarrow G
\]

inside one system.

### 4.3 Scale-dependent effective degrees of freedom — Established

Renormalization-group and effective-field-theory methods explicitly connect descriptions valid at different scales.

Interacting systems can develop collective phenomena, bound states, and new effective degrees of freedom as energy scale changes.

RRG's stronger claim is that a common resonant-geometry recursion might underlie some of these transitions.

### 4.4 Whole constraining parts — Established as a complex-systems pattern

Synergetics describes macroscopic order parameters arising from microscopic components and subsequently constraining (“enslaving”) component behavior.

This closely matches RRG's circular:

\[
\text{parts}\rightarrow\text{whole}\rightarrow\text{parts}
\]

### 4.5 Geometry changes spectra and forces — Established in specific systems

Boundary geometry affects mode spectra.

Casimir physics is a concrete example where boundary geometry and spectral structure are directly linked to measurable forces.

This does **not** imply that all fundamental forces are Casimir-like.

It demonstrates only that:

\[
\text{geometry}
\rightarrow
\text{spectrum}
\rightarrow
\text{effective force}
\]

is physically possible.

### 4.6 Autocatalytic closure and self-reproduction — Established research framework

Origin-of-life work on autocatalytic sets formally studies networks in which products collectively catalyze reactions needed to sustain the network.

RAF theory formalizes “catalytic closure” and gives a mathematically precise bridge between ordinary chemistry and self-sustaining chemical organization.

RRG interprets biological reproduction as a highly developed form of propagation of dynamic organization.

### 4.7 Neural manifolds — Established research framework

Large neural populations often occupy lower-dimensional collective state spaces constrained by intrinsic connectivity and behavioral context.

This is strongly compatible with treating cognition as trajectories through dynamic relational geometry.

## 5. Scale and time

RRG predicts that a higher-level structure should generally have its own characteristic timescale:

\[
\tau_n
\]

It is tempting to say “larger = always slower”, but that is too strong.

A safer relation is:

\[
\tau\sim
\frac{L}{v_{\text{effective}}}
\times
(\text{coordination complexity})
\]

where:

- \(L\) is a characteristic length,
- \(v_{\text{effective}}\) is a propagation/coupling speed,
- additional collective processes can introduce longer timescales.

**RRG hypothesis:** recursive organization may often create increasing separation of characteristic timescales across levels.

This must be measured rather than assumed universally.

Life requires continual work to preserve far-from-equilibrium organization.

## 11. The four interactions: current RRG position

RRG must **not** assume in advance:

- strong = scale 1,
- weak = scale 2,
- electromagnetic = scale 3,
- gravity = scale 4.

Known physics does not organize that cleanly.

Electromagnetism and the weak interaction already unify into electroweak theory at sufficiently high energies.

The strong interaction has scale-dependent behavior.

Gravity dominates astronomical organization while being extremely weak between individual particles.

Therefore the more defensible RRG hypothesis is:

> **Different scales and stable geometries can generate or expose different effective interaction regimes. The known fundamental interactions may or may not ultimately be derivable from one deeper geometry–mode law.**

This remains one of the central open tests.

## 12. Gravity: hardest open physics problem for RRG

For RRG to derive gravity, it must eventually recover at least:

- universal free fall / equivalence principle,
- Lorentz-compatible causal structure,
- weak-field Newtonian limit,
- gravitational redshift,
- lensing,
- relativistic orbital dynamics,
- gravitational waves,
- black-hole solutions.

Analogue-gravity systems show that effective curved metrics and horizons can emerge from underlying collective media.

That demonstrates logical possibility, not that real spacetime is such a medium.

RRG should treat gravity as **open**, not solved.

## 13. Thermodynamics constraint

RRG cannot say “complexity naturally increases” without qualification.

The second law still constrains physical processes.

Local organization can increase when energy and entropy are exported.

Therefore any RRG simulation of self-maintaining higher levels needs:

- energy input,
- dissipation,
- entropy accounting,
- finite resources,
- instability/death pathways.

A useful RRG distinction is:

- **passively persistent structures** — bound states that need no continuous external power to remain in their state;
- **actively maintained structures** — far-from-equilibrium systems requiring ongoing energy flow.

## 15. What would falsify or weaken RRG

RRG becomes scientifically useful only if it can fail.

Strong failure modes include:

1. **No recursive closure:** every scale requires an unrelated hand-written interaction law.
2. **No coarse-grained form invariance:** after promoting a stable cluster to a supernode, the governing equation cannot remain in the same mathematical family even approximately.
3. **No spontaneous hierarchy:** hierarchy appears only because the researcher explicitly programs levels.
4. **Wrong known physics:** the model predicts violations already ruled out experimentally.
5. **No novel prediction:** the language only redescribes known outcomes after they happen.
6. **Trivial universality:** “geometry” and “mode” are defined so broadly that every possible result fits automatically.

The framework should be constrained enough to avoid #6.

# v0.1a Addendum — Scale Ladder and Latent Interaction Channels

## 18. Corrected observational ladder

RRG should not use an incorrect chronology to support the hypothesis.

A defensible simplified ladder is:

1. **Very early hot field/particle regime** — including a quark–gluon plasma in the first microseconds.
2. **Hadrons** — quarks become confined into composite hadrons such as protons and neutrons.
3. **Big-bang nucleosynthesis** — mostly hydrogen and helium nuclei, with traces of light nuclei.
4. **Early molecular chemistry** — helium hydride \(HeH^+\) is expected to have been the first molecular species, around \(10^5\) years after the Big Bang.
5. **Neutral atoms / recombination** — mainly hydrogen and helium atoms, around \(3.8\times10^5\) years.
6. **First stars** — gravitational collapse of primordial H/He gas, roughly hundreds of millions of years after the Big Bang.
7. **Stellar nucleosynthesis and stellar explosions** — production/distribution of many heavier elements.
8. **Later stars, disks, rocky planets, oceans/atmospheres, rich chemistry.**
9. **Prebiotic chemistry** — networks involving nucleotides, peptides, amphiphiles, catalytic surfaces, energy gradients, and compartmentalization.
10. **Self-maintaining / replicating chemical systems and protocells** — historical path still unresolved.
11. **Cells → multicellularity → nervous systems → cognition → humans.**
12. **Technology → externally constructed information-processing systems → AI.**

This ladder is evidence for repeated emergence of new effective units, but it does **not** by itself prove that a single mathematical recursion generates every level.

## 19. A better formulation of “the force is already there waiting”

The conversational intuition was:

> A new scale/geometry eventually becomes capable of resonating with an interaction that was already possible, and that interaction becomes important at that level.

The mathematically safer version is **latent interaction channels**.

At scale \(k\), write the effective action schematically as:

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_k[R]
=
\Gamma_{0,k}[R]
+
\sum_{\alpha}g_\alpha(k)\,\mathcal O_\alpha[R]
}
\]

where:

- \(R=(G,M)\) is the current resonant geometry/state;
- \(\mathcal O_\alpha\) is an allowed interaction channel/operator;
- \(g_\alpha(k)\) is the scale-dependent strength of that channel.

The allowed channels are constrained by symmetry, topology, locality, conservation laws, and the underlying microscopic theory.

Their strengths need not be constant:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{dg_\alpha}{d\ln k}
=
\beta_\alpha(\{g\})
}
\]

So an interaction can be negligible at one scale, relevant at another, dominant at another, or reorganized after a symmetry-breaking/phase transition.

This gives a precise interpretation of “waiting”:

> the channel need not be literally absent; it may be dynamically suppressed, short-ranged, screened, confined, or irrelevant until the scale and background geometry make it important.

### Known examples

#### QCD / strong interaction

At high energies, the strong interaction is weaker; at lower energies it becomes strong enough that quarks/gluons form hadrons. Hadronization is energy-scale dependent.

This is an excellent example of:

\[
\boxed{
\text{same underlying interaction}
+
\text{different scale}
\rightarrow
\text{different effective geometry/objects}
}
\]

#### Electroweak interaction

At high energy the weak and electromagnetic interactions are described by one electroweak theory.

After the Higgs field settles into a symmetry-breaking vacuum, the \(W\) and \(Z\) acquire mass while the photon remains massless. Their effective ranges and low-energy behavior then differ strongly.

This is a concrete case where a **background field configuration changes the observable interaction regime**.

#### Gravity

Gravity is not experimentally known to “switch on” only at large scale. It is present at small scales too, but is extraordinarily weak there.

It becomes dominant for astronomical structures because it is long-range, universally attractive in ordinary situations, and large masses can accumulate without electric-charge cancellation.

RRG may eventually attempt to derive gravity as an emergent collective geometry, but that remains an open hypothesis.

# v0.1b Addendum — Background Selection, Scale, and the Remaining Blockers

## 21. Background-selection principle

RRG now treats the effective theory as background dependent:

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_k[R;B(t)]
}
\]

where \(B(t)\) is the slowly varying global or environmental background. Depending on application, \(B\) may include:

- cosmic scale factor,
- temperature,
- density,
- curvature,
- background field expectation values,
- pressure,
- chemical environment,
- external energy flux,
- boundary conditions.

Stable resonant geometries are solutions of:

\[
\boxed{
\delta_R\Gamma_k[R;B]=0
}
\]

with an additional stability requirement from the fluctuation spectrum.

As \(B\) changes, the number, identity, and stability of solutions can change through phase transitions, bifurcations, or symmetry breaking:

\[
\boxed{
B_1\rightarrow B_2
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mathcal S(B_1)\rightarrow\mathcal S(B_2)
}
\]

This is RRG's precise replacement for saying that a later structure was “waiting”.

### Established analogues

- Cosmic expansion stretches wavelengths and changes the thermodynamic background.
- The early Universe cooled through regimes in which hadrons, nuclei, and later neutral atoms became possible.
- Electroweak symmetry breaking changes the realized particle spectrum and interaction ranges without requiring the deepest equations to be rewritten.

These are analogues of the background-selection mechanism, not proof of the complete RRG recursion.

## 22. Scale as a geometry–mode regime

RRG should not define scale by length alone.

A scale is provisionally a regime in which a collective structure can be treated as one effective degree of freedom:

\[
\boxed{
R_n=(G_n,M_n;B_n)
}
\]

with characteristic:

\[
L_n,\quad \tau_n,\quad \Omega_n,\quad \{g_{\alpha,n}\}.
\]

Lower-level modes remain active internally. The higher-level collective mode determines the effective interactions visible at the next description level.

This avoids the incorrect claim that lower physics disappears when a higher level emerges.

## 23. What remains genuinely unresolved

The conceptual loop is now largely closed. The remaining blockers are quantitative:

### A. One scale-closed effective action

Find a nontrivial \(\Gamma_k\) whose mathematical family survives repeated coarse-graining:

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_n\xrightarrow{\mathcal R}\Gamma_{n+1}
}
\]

without manually inserting a new law at each level.

### B. Automatic composite promotion

A new effective unit must emerge from the dynamics themselves, for example through a long-lived pole, isolated collective mode, spectral gap, or stable attractor. The researcher should not manually declare “this is now a particle/cell/node”.

### C. Recover known fundamental constraints

Any fundamental version must reproduce, at minimum, the experimentally tested structures of quantum field theory and gravity: Lorentz symmetry, gauge structure, spin/statistics, known particle properties, conservation laws, equivalence principle, and the Newtonian/GR limits.

### D. Thermodynamics and open systems

The same framework must support both passive bound structures and actively maintained dissipative structures such as cells and brains without violating energy conservation or the second law.

### E. A prediction unique to RRG

RRG must predict at least one stable geometry, transition threshold, scaling relation, or interaction regime not inserted as an assumption and distinguishable from existing theories.

### F. Avoiding triviality

If “geometry” means every relation and “mode” means every possible change, all observations fit automatically. RRG must remain restrictive enough that some possible systems are forbidden or unstable by the equations.

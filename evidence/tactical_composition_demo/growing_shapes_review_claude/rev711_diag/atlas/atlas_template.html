Growing Shapes Atlas

Real recorded positions from the F5 growth test. Eight sensor sites sit on a ring of radius 4 m.u.; the output O must be reached by a chain of strong links from a cell the sensors drive. Each panel is one moment of one run; drag the scrubber to move through time.

What a shape is

A cell is an oscillator with a position and a phase (the small tick). Cells joined by strong links (coupling ≥ 0.5 per second) form a shape; each shape gets its own colour. Grey cells belong to no multi-cell shape. The orange chain is a strong path from a driven cell to O, the connection the task needs.

Why the link snaps back

Each cell moves toward the average of its eight nearest neighbours. A cell at the tip of a bridge has about seven neighbours behind it and only O in front, so it is pulled back into the cluster at 0.3–0.6 m.u. per second. The cluster behaves like a water drop: it stays round. Moving O toward the first driven cell (the root-relative pin) lets the cluster's resting edge reach O, which is why that run keeps its path.

Measured across five seed sets: the root-relative pin passes the F5 thresholds in 8 of 10 cases, the centre pin in 3 of 10. Spring links between linked cells are being tested now.

Where this is heading: shapes as nodes

Inside a shape: stable and organized. Connections only at its ports. A shape that does one job becomes one node at the next level, and requests reach the right shape by resonance.

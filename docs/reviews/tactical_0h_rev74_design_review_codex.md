2. **R74-2 — LOW: 256/s is a single-drive coefficient bound, not a conservative stiffness bound for the coupled system.**

   **Evidence:** D:31 sums site drives and includes internal coupling; D:500 uses only λgk = 32·2·4. At fixed geometry, write C_i = K mean_j exp(−r_ij²) ≤ 1 and D_i = g_i Σ_s k_s K_d(r_is). The phase Jacobian row's absolute sum is bounded by λ(2C_i + D_i), because internal coupling contributes both its diagonal derivative and off-diagonal derivatives. Even the one-site envelope gives 32(2+8) = 320/s, not 256/s. Multiple reachable sites require their weighted sum; drive mean-normalization is not present. This phase-only bound still omits phase–position cross derivatives, element-pair motion near small separations, and neighbour/reach switching. D:353's site-body bound is explicitly a new law; it is not a full-system bound either.

   **Fix:** call 256/s the **one-site maximum drive derivative scale**. Give the phase-only bound above, or a tighter bound using the declared physical-site layout, and explicitly distinguish it from a full coupled/hybrid stability guarantee. For the isolated decaying scalar mode, h·256 = 1.28 is safely inside classical RK4's negative-real interval (approximately 2.785); the one-site phase envelope gives h·320 = 1.6. Both support trying h = 0.005. Stability is not accuracy: R_RK4(z) = 1+z+z²/2+z³/6+z⁴/24, and comparison with exp(z) is a separate question. Complex eigenvalues require the full stability region. This derivation uses the [UCLA numerical-analysis stability notes](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~anderson/270e.1.08f/summaries/RegionsOfAbsoluteStability.html), which define the scalar test equation and RK4 polynomial. N1, not the scalar estimate, remains the admission gate; no present observation establishes instability at h = 0.005.

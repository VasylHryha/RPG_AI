## Findings and concrete fixes

### R3-1 — MEDIUM, before sizing: failure scenarios and the shared-n rule are not fully specified

Section 9.3, lines 191–218. R-a/R-b/R-c all have mean 0.70; N-a/N-b/N-c all have mean 0.85. R-a and N-a attain the maximum variance at their respective means on [0,1]. The independent-orientation probabilities are correct. R-b is a declared development-like scenario, not the actual empirical distribution (14/4/2 of 20 regular clusters have w=1/0.5/0). Maximum variance does not by itself prove minimum power for this adaptive betting test; taking the minimum across the declared scenarios is the right conditional safeguard.

But a marginal failure rate f=0.02 does not define R-d or N-d. The draft does not specify whether failures are independent between orientations, correlated within a cluster, or dependent on whether the fight would have won. Those alternatives change the post-penalty distribution and power.

For example, start from R-a and independently replace each oriented outcome with a failure-coded zero with probability 0.02, independent of the underlying outcome. The resulting probabilities at w=1, 0.5, 0 are **0.67228, 0.02744, 0.30028**, mean **0.686**. Instead, fail both orientations together with probability 0.02, independent of the underlying cluster: the probabilities are **0.686, 0, 0.314**, the same mean with a different variance. Concentrating a 2% per-fight failure rate on otherwise winning fights produces mean 0.68. All are compatible with the present marginal description. These are analytical examples, not simulated power estimates or recommended replacement assumptions.

The selection rule also takes the larger of the two heads' separately minimal qualifying n. The bet itself changes with n. No proof establishes that a head passing at a smaller n must pass at the other head's selected larger n, and finite Monte Carlo lower bounds can cross non-monotonically even if true power increases. The displayed common n must actually satisfy every required cell at that n.

**Fix:** freeze the oriented win/failure generation law, including dependence, and print its resulting cluster probabilities and means. Then select the **smallest common candidate n whose lower power bounds pass all four scenarios for both heads at that same n**. Stop if no common candidate qualifies. Do not change the assumptions or simulation seed after seeing the table.

The Clopper–Pearson allocation over 24 cells **per head** gives at least 99% simultaneous Monte Carlo coverage separately for each head; across all 48 cells it gives at least 98% by a union bound. This does not damage judging alpha and is not intrinsically invalid. If the shared sizing claim is intended to have 99% simultaneous assurance for both heads, allocate the 0.01 Monte Carlo error over **48** cells (one-sided tail 0.01/48), or explicitly state the existing per-head assurance and joint limit. Freeze the CP tail, zero/all-success conventions, implementation/version, independent replications within each cell, scenario/input hashes and exact RNG consumption/order. Dependence between cells does not invalidate Bonferroni.

### R3-2 — MEDIUM, before freezing sizing code: valid construction, incorrect exact-paper attribution

Section 9.2, lines 177–189. The mean estimate has the paper's initialization and indexing. The variance estimate does not: the paper's equation (26) uses residual **X_j − mu_hat_j**, whereas the proposal uses **X_j − mu_hat_(j−1)**. For a first observation X_1=1, the paper has mu_hat_1=0.75 and variance estimate **5/32**; the proposal has variance estimate **1/4**. Later bets and finite-sample power can differ. [Primary paper, equation (26), Theorem 3 and Remark 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09686).

**This is not an invalidity finding.** In either variant, the estimate used for bet i depends only on observations before i. It stays positive because of its prior term. Theorem 3 permits predictable base bets independent of m; h=1 retains only positive capital. The proposed final K_n, rather than a running intersection, and log(1/alpha), rather than the two-sided recommendation's log(2/alpha), are valid declared one-sided choices. They need not reproduce the paper's recommended two-sided fixed-n procedure.

**Fix:** either use the paper's residual X_j − mu_hat_j, or retain the presently written valid estimator and explicitly label it a variant. Correct the claim that it is exactly the paper's plug-in. Choose once **before** implementing/freezing the shared sizing and judging decision code; do not silently substitute a package default. No new method search is needed.

My validity check is direct: each factor is at least 1−c=1/2, and, under conditional mean at most m, its conditional expectation is 1+lambda_i(m)(E[X_i|past]−m)≤1. Thus E[K_n(m)]≤1 and rejecting at K_n(m)≥1/alpha has probability at most alpha. Under the intended i.i.d. cluster sampling this covers the composite null mean≤m. The descriptive S transformation (S+50)/100 has the same bounded range when applied to a completely defined score sample.

The grid is also sound in exact arithmetic. Write a_i for the predictable positive base bet. An unclipped factor is 1+a_i X_i−a_i m; a clipped factor is 1−c+c X_i/m. Both decrease or stay constant as m increases, agree at the clipping boundary, and remain positive. Their product is therefore nonincreasing. The last rejecting grid point cannot skip an unrejected interval below it and rounds the exact lower boundary down. **Do not report the first nonrejecting grid point as the lower bound.** Strict LB>0.5 on a 10^-4 grid requires a rejecting point at least 0.5001; it is slightly more conservative than rejecting only at 0.5.

Before sealing, define LB=0 if no positive grid point rejects, boundary evaluation at m=0/1, finite-input validation and conservative numerical comparison of log capital to log(1/alpha). Downward grid rounding alone does not certify floating-point capital calculations near equality. Use a justified enclosure or a deterministic higher-precision/interval check for ambiguous comparisons; never add an outcome-selected epsilon or fallback. The all-win lower bound remains below 1 for finite n; all-loss data do not support the endpoint.

**Blocking — ECO-F does not define the deficit comparison that controls retraction.** Economy spec lines 18–21 say that the B-path directed deficit "decreased at the last check", without identifying the two compared snapshots. RD3's `b_path()` computes the directed forward-to-backward deficit before its births and only for active, unserved sites (`rev7_design.py:427–431`); its final graph is used for path/waiting status, not a post-birth deficit (`447–466`). `service_snapshot()` supplies all-site roots, but its `gaps` are distances from roots to O's undirected spring component (`service_graph.py:59–71`), explicitly a different quantity. Neither existing module supplies the specified all-physical-site progress history.

A concrete counterexample: one check starts with deficit 2.0 and its birth leaves 1.0; the next check starts at 1.5 and leaves 1.5. Comparing successive pre-birth deficits says the second check improved (2.0 → 1.5); comparing its own before/after deficits says it stalled (1.5 → 1.5). The two interpretations reset different clocks and delete different bodies. Reading the observer's spring-gap samples would implement a third law. This is an arm-meaning decision, not an instrumentation detail an implementer should silently choose.

The drafter must specify the comparison snapshots and stage, all-site directed computation independent of activity, initial history and absent-output/root/infinite transitions. The per-boundary use of "at the last check" also needs an explicit persistence rule: literally, a successful last-check predicate resets at every 0.1-second boundary until the next check; a one-shot reset starts accumulating immediately. Those differ by a full growth interval. Define whether progress from an earlier check can reset a clock that has since lost and regained roots. Keep the clock and its history kernel-owned and clone-copied, independent of observer state.

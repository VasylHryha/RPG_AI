1. **High — The two enemy movement laws disagree, and the proposed retreat reverses the range-restoring force.**

   `DESIGN_0G.md:71-77` first gives a factor `1 + J_e cos(phi_e - theta_i)`, then replaces it in prose with `(1 + J_e cos theta_i)(1 - J_f cos phi_e)`. These differ even in simple states: at `theta_i=0, phi_e=pi`, the first is `1-J_e`, while the second is `(1+J_e)(1+J_f)`. An implementer cannot satisfy both.

   In the prose version, isolate one enemy, hold the phases fixed and write the enemy-directed radial speed as `c(1-d/r)`. For the intended retreat state, `J_e>1` makes `c<0` when the other factors are positive. With a stationary enemy, `r_dot=-c(1-d/r)`: just outside `d` the unit retreats farther; just inside `d` it moves **toward** the enemy. Thus `r=d` becomes unstable precisely when retreat is requested. At `J_e<=1`, the commitment factor at theta=pi cannot reverse long-range attraction into retreat; ordinary close-range repulsion remains possible. If `J_f>1`, its enemy-state factor introduces another sign reversal. This is a mathematical counterexample for a held phase and an isolated interaction, not a measured gameplay failure or proof that every multi-unit trajectory diverges.

   **Fix:** choose one equation. Keep close-range repulsion independent of the sign of commitment; use a bounded phase-dependent preferred distance with a nonnegative restoring gain, or a separate outward retreat term. Specify gain bounds and centre-distance versus surface-gap conversion. Check the signs inside, at and outside the preferred distance before fight development. The bridge uses surface gap for melee/direct and centre distance plus minimum range for artillery (`astelia_cpp/src/native/controller_bridge.cpp:51-53`); these are different units of reach.

2. **High — The damage measurement contract disagrees with the plug, and the locking conditions are unstated.**

   `DESIGN_0G.md:25,53-67` leaves “exponential average” ambiguous between recent HP, HP per second and an average of per-tick increments. These produce different gains when `dt` changes. The current plug supplies **cumulative** HP damage, not recent averages (`astelia_cpp/src/native/controller.h:16-18`, `controller_bridge.cpp:33-35`). Its counters include friendly damage: `World::damage` increments them without testing the source/target teams (`world.cpp:211-226`), and game shots and lobs can hit allies (`combat.cpp:55-60,144-148`). Consequently the claimed enemy-only exchange cannot be reconstructed exactly from these two counters.

   Algebraically the two drive terms equal `D_i sin(theta_i)`, where `D_i=kappa_in*in_i-kappa_out*out_i`. Equal weighted incoming/outgoing damage cancels. That is a valid net-pressure design choice; cancellation is not by itself a defect or a violation of the owner's request for both inputs. With no other coupling and constant inputs, `theta_dot=omega+D sin(theta)`. It is a smooth, well-defined ODE for finite bounded inputs. For `omega=0`, `D<0` attracts toward 0 and `D>0` toward pi, but the opposite phase is also an exact stationary point. With nonzero omega, locking requires `|D|>=|omega|` and the equilibrium is generally offset from 0 or pi; at equality it is marginal. For `|omega|>|D|` the phase keeps rotating. Thus neither numerical instability nor guaranteed retreat follows merely from the drive's form. Nonzero natural rate is drift, not relaxation toward a reference (`:67`).

   **Fix:** write the recurrence and units. For example, `q=exp(-dt/T_d)` and `z_next=q*z+(1-q)*(C_next-C_prev)/dt` defines an HP/s average; dividing by max HP gives 1/s and fixes the gain's units. Require `T_d>0`, positive max HP, finite bounded parameters, initial averages and an explicit enemy-phase ODE/initialization. Call enemy phi an inferred damage-state phase: it is controller memory, not a measured oscillator inside the scripted opponent. Either declare all-source damage as the signal, or provide cross-team counters identically to all arms. Retain dealt and taken in diagnostics, but state that this particular drive uses their weighted difference. Document the locking condition instead of promising that every hit causes retreat.

3. **High — Target-group phase is circularly defined unless updates are ordered, and it does not guarantee distinct groups or focus fire.**

   `DESIGN_0G.md:53,61,80-84` uses current target assignments to construct `psi_j`, then uses `psi_j` to choose targets. It gives a fallback for nobody targeting `j`, but none for a nonempty group with a zero resultant: phases 0 and pi make `arg(sum exp(i theta))` undefined. A sole attacker contributes itself, giving `psi_j=theta_i` and zero target-coupling torque. A connected set of similar units with positive coupling and common drive can synchronize globally; if several occupied targets then have the same group phase, the alignment score cannot distinguish those groups. Conversely disconnected neighborhoods can retain distinct phases. Neither multiple phase labels nor universal collapse follows from this law.

   Identical phases also do not imply identical targets when units have different reachable sets or retain different targets through hysteresis. Any shared tie-break can itself produce focus. These qualifications matter because `:81` presents focus as a consequence of synchronization.

   **Fix:** use previous-tick target assignments for the phase step, compute all new targets from one frozen prepared state, and specify whether the querying unit contributes to each candidate's phase. Define a small-resultant fallback, deterministic ties, hysteresis units, and behavior when no target is valid. Log phase coherence, distinct occupied-target phases and target concentration separately. Treat global synchrony as an allowed outcome, not evidence for several functional groups. Remove the guaranteed-focus sentence until development demonstrates the mechanism.

4. **High — “Plain morale” and “push-pull” are descriptions, not controlled comparisons.**

   `DESIGN_0G.md:105-114` supplies no morale dynamics, bounds, enemy update, pair-similarity function, target-group aggregation or readout. A circular phase can already be represented by one real number with periodic functions. Simply saying “one real number” does not remove a beat. Conversely replacing a phase by `cos(theta)` loses phase information and changing the readout can handicap the comparison independently of smoothing. P2 cannot currently separate circular dynamics from damage smoothing, neighborhood averaging, targeting and range control.

   Push-pull additionally changes target selection to nearest. A loss against the resonator could therefore reflect focus/target ranking rather than movement or phase dynamics. It is a reasonable potential-field baseline, but beating it would not establish superiority to physicomimetics generally. The original [Spears et al. paper](https://www.clear.rice.edu/comp551/papers/Spears-Physicomimetics-AR2004.pdf), pp. 138-140, specifies force laws, force/speed limits, neighborhood sensing and a velocity/friction update; “forces only” does not identify that algorithm. The already acknowledged [swarmalator model](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-01190-3), equations 1-4, has bidirectional position/phase coupling. A force-only comparison does not test novelty relative to that family.

   **Fix:** give each arm equations and an exact action rule before coding. Match observation history, damage averaging, neighbors, legal actions, range handling, hysteresis and update timing wherever they are not the contrast. Define a nonperiodic morale state, its scale and similarity kernel explicitly. Prefer retaining the owner's whole-controller comparison and narrowing P3 to superiority over this specified push-pull baseline. A separate comparison with a shared target rule would isolate movement, but is not required for the owner's v0 goal. Keeping four arms is sufficient; no new literature benchmark is necessary if the claims are correspondingly narrow. With the owner's diagnostic-only ablations, P2/P3 do not independently establish that both directions of the geometry/mode loop are necessary. Likewise, a circular state that settles to a static phase can outperform morale without demonstrating that ongoing oscillation—the beat in a temporal sense—is necessary; distinguish circular state, phase coupling and sustained rhythm in the claims.

5. **High — The panel and sampling unit are undefined for P1, so “114 fights” is not yet an inferential sample size.**

   `PLAN_RESONATOR_AI.md:58,99,108-111` combines a frozen ladder with “19 opponents x 3 seeds x 2 sides = 114,” then requires 114 wins/losses **against novice** and another 114 **against regular**. The existing 19-member pool consists of doctrines/formations, not difficulty levels (`astelia_cpp/src/native/api.cpp:3-7`). The previous composition work compares novice/regular scores against that pool (`astelia_compose/README.md:8-12`). This makes reuse of a common-pool score comparison plausible, but the 0g wording does not specify it. Outperforming the novice's pool score is not directly beating novice in a fight. Separately instantiating 19 profiles at each difficulty needs an exact profile table: applying `level=regular` resets the base formation to line unless an explicit formation overrides it (`config_codec.cpp:244-248`), so some labels could describe duplicate matchups rather than diversity. If the intended panel instead contains one novice and one regular entry, each has only six fights; even 6/6 wins has one-sided null probability `2^-6=0.015625`, insufficient at alpha 0.01 before multiplicity. That six-fight case is a conditional warning, not a claim about the current pool's contents.

   Both orientations of the same seed share a starting world. Reusing the same three seeds across opponents adds further dependence. Clopper-Pearson is a binomial construction, not a justification for treating repeated orientations or configurations as independent trials. The [NIST definition](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/exacbici.htm) also distinguishes one-sided bounds from two-sided intervals. Neither tail convention nor the exact hypothesis family is frozen here.

   **Fix:** define the matchup table and claim population: direct novice and regular panels for P1, and a specified weighted opponent pool for P2/P3. State which draws are independent, which seeds are reused, and how orientation pairs are aggregated or clustered. Join paired arm results by opponent configuration, seed and orientation, never array position. Freeze the inferential method, tail convention, multiplicity allocation and draw/timeout treatment. Preserve mirrored fights for side balance, but do not count them as independent merely because there are two outcomes.

7. **Medium — P1 is possible but poorly powered near 60%; P2/P3 attainability must wait for paired development noise.**

   `PLAN_RESONATOR_AI.md:103,108-115` correctly schedules noise measurement before registration, but “114” cannot already promise attainability. As an illustrative **independent, identical Bernoulli** calculation, 60% requires 69/114 wins; its one-sided tail at `p=0.5` is about 0.01539, so it fails even unsplit alpha 0.01. At unsplit 0.01 the critical count is 70. If the four elementary tests are novice, regular, morale and push-pull, an equal one-sided allocation of 0.0025 needs 73 wins (64.0%). Its power at a true win probability of 0.60 is only about 21.7%; at 0.70 it is about 93.0%. These are conditional arithmetic, not power estimates for the actual dependent panel. The P1 wording establishes empirical 60% and evidence above 50%, not a population win rate above 60%.

   The four-way allocation is **not compulsory**. If the inferential family has only P1, P2 and P3, with P1 supported only when both novice and regular subtests pass, allocate 0.01/3 to each endpoint and use that allocation for each P1 subtest. Under the P1 null, at least one component is false; the chance both tests pass is at most the false component's rejection probability. This intersection-union argument controls the joint P1 claim without another division by two; it does not license separate endpoint claims when only one opponent passes. In the same ideal binomial example, 0.01/3 needs 72/114 wins and has about 27.8% power at p=0.60 **per opponent**. Joint P1 power is no greater than either component's power and depends on their dependence. This alternative remains poorly powered near 60%, but avoids an unnecessary restriction. The underlying principle is described in [intersection-union testing research](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167715210003445).

   For independent paired margin differences with SD `s_D`, a normal approximation at one-sided 0.0025 gives a lower-bound penalty about `2.807*s_D/sqrt(114)=0.263*s_D`. Approximately 80% power needs a true excess above delta of `0.342*s_D`. For example, `s_D=10` survivors requires an excess about 3.42 survivors, whereas `s_D=20` requires about 6.83. If there are 57 independent orientation-pair averages, the corresponding 80% coefficient is about 0.483 times **that aggregate's** SD. Finite-sample, cluster and nonnormal methods can differ; these examples are not registered tests.

   **Fix:** at S4 estimate the spread of the actual paired/clustered comparison on development data and a separate development validation split, including raw outcomes and uncertainty. Keep a meaningful owner-approved delta in survivor units; do not shrink it simply to obtain a pass. Before S5 either select an adequate final sample size from a bounded power rule or retain 114 with explicit low-power/INDETERMINATE expectations. Do not estimate sample size from final fights or extend a panel after seeing its result. No development execution is authorized by this review.

9. **Medium — Scaling and RK4 matching are useful engineering checks, but do not establish sound in-game C4 dynamics.**

   `DESIGN_0G.md:45,60,62,75,78,129-131` defines `L`, a time knob `tau`, a fixed model step 0.02 and a goal horizon `h` without specifying their relationship to game `dt`. `PLAN_RESONATOR_AI.md:20` says ten decisions per second, while the bridge prepares and decides every physics tick (`astelia_cpp/src/native/combat.cpp:84-86,115-116`).    **Fix:** add the normalization ledger: `r=distance_px/L`, preferred distances in the same model units, damage in HP/s or the chosen alternative, rates/gains per stated time unit. 11. **Medium — Degenerate states, sanitized failures and the second score need exact definitions.**

   `DESIGN_0G.md:53,61,71-80,99-103` does not define empty means, coincident positions, target loss or damage tie-breaks. The C4 implementation defines no-neighbor motion as zero, uses a protected distance and normalizes by at least one (`geomind/c4_model.py:8-11,76-79,87-101`). The new enemy term has its own divisions by distance. “Enemies in reach” must also exclude the artillery dead zone. An isolated unit should still have intrinsic/damage phase dynamics and enemy-driven movement; once all enemies die, no target-dependent torque or old target should remain.

   Two existing engine behaviors deserve explicit treatment. First, a non-finite decision silently becomes hold/no target (`controller_bridge.cpp:42-45`, tested at `native_controller_contract.cpp:49-51`). An arm can finish 114 fights “with no error” despite recurring numerical failures; the host need not report an exception. Second, `summary.total` and `summary.enemyDamage` aggregate damage by source side, including friendly damage (`api.cpp:17`, `world.cpp:225-238`). Using their difference as the requested exchange score would credit our own friendly-fire damage positively. A tie on survivors could therefore be broken in favor of an arm for hurting its allies. These are concrete counter/validation semantics, not hypothetical changes to game rules.

   **Fix:** define empty sums/means as zero, finite handling of zero distance, no-target `K_t` as zero, stale/dead ID removal, target=none when no legal enemy is reachable, and terminal behavior. Specify a policy for enemies symmetrically cancelling the mean force. Use the bridge's role-specific legality, including artillery minimum range, rather than a single reach test. Have each new controller validate finite internal state/raw actions before the bridge, with a failure receipt; distinguish invalid numeric actions from ordinary declared boundary clipping. Such failures must not become normal win/loss data or disappear by dropping a seed. Add these cases to the future S3 change batch. For the score, freeze a lexicographic fight outcome `(survivor difference, damage difference)`, including exact ties/timeouts, or explicitly state that damage is descriptive only for registered survivor endpoints. Define actual cross-team HP damage and any separate friendly-fire penalty, including dead attackers' contributions, rather than blindly subtracting the existing summary totals. Never resolve a survivor-based statistical tie post hoc using whichever damage statistic looks favorable.

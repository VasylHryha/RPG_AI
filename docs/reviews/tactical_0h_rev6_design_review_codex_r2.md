## Numbered findings

### 1. R2-1 — HIGH: B-path can accept a birth that does not extend the directed frontier

**Evidence:** R6:83–90 requires only clearance and `distance(newborn, b) < distance(a, b)`. In C4:71–79, the receiver selects its eight nearest neighbors; `a → newborn` exists only if **the newborn selects a**. Eight closer nodes outside F can exclude a, even when the newborn is exactly r* from a and closer to b. Copying θ_a is an initialization, not an ongoing influence edge. Inserting a node can also displace existing neighbors and destroy a previously reachable route. Nothing in the acceptance rule tests either effect.

The shortest F/T pair need not admit this construction. R6:90 logs `saturated` and stops for distances below r*, rather than trying a different pair. A site with another feasible frontier can therefore remain blocked. If B-out fails and O is empty, T is empty and step 2 has no pair to select. If all clearance alternatives fail, the terminal outcome is not specified. Low site ids can consume both successful insertion slots at every check while later disconnected sites wait indefinitely.

**Fix:** trial each candidate under the actual post-insertion neighbor graph. Require a declared directed progress measure: at minimum the newborn must be forward-reachable from the intended site's roots, existing required reachability must be preserved, and a global F-to-T deficit must strictly improve or the site must reach O. Define the measure and bounded alternate-pair/direction search before data. Specify empty O/F/T, exhausted clearance, saturated frontiers and all rejection categories. Register a deterministic fair site scheduler, or explicitly measure and bound the starvation accepted by the policy. A geometric distance decrease alone must not be called directed progress.

### 2. R2-2 — MEDIUM: r* improves a local coefficient; it does not answer the bandwidth finding

**Evidence:** R6:86 calls `exp(−r*²) ≈ 0.73` an answer to C3. C4:100–101 divides that term by the receiver's neighbor count. With eight neighbors one link's maximum contribution is about 0.092 rad/s. Its isolated, zero-detuning small-error time scale is about 10.9 s; that is a local estimate, not a multi-link delay or a guarantee. Clipped intrinsic rates can differ by π rad/s. Competing neighbors can further cancel the useful contribution.

r* is an **in-phase pair** equilibrium, not an equilibrium of a driven multi-neighbor chain carrying different input phases. Furthermore B-path stops as soon as any topological path exists: a pre-existing path with long, weak edges gets no repair. R6:94 correctly admits the remaining uncertainty, but F1 has no registered input change, chain length, detuning, accuracy tolerance or delay criterion. An initially phase-matched chain can appear to follow a constant carrier without transmitting a new observation.

**Fix:** retain the shorter spacing as a hypothesis and remove the assertion that it closes C3. Register waveform changes after initialization, relevant path lengths, receiver counts, competing inputs and detuning cases, along with a numerical response tolerance and deadline within the task interval. Measure actual link weights/counts, path persistence and response delay. Decide whether weak-but-connected paths trigger growth or simply fail the engineering gate. No frozen-C4 change is required merely to make this uncertainty explicit.

### 5. R2-5 — MEDIUM: the B-out coherence estimator and timer are ambiguous

**Evidence:** R6:36 defines instantaneous spatial coherence `|mean_O exp(iθ)|`. R6:75 instead uses “output coherence over the last 100 samples,” without a formula, missing-history rule or changing-membership convention. Mean instantaneous coherence, coherence of a time-averaged complex signal, and carrier-demodulated temporal coherence are different quantities. Averaging the raw complex signal over 10 s can cancel an ideal π-rad/s carrier.

If the intended statistic is instantaneous C or its sample mean, one output has C = 1 at every frame. Starting from O empty, the first accepted B-out birth makes |O| = 1 and the low-coherence rule can never create a second living output. This also fixes perceive's magnitude at zero through the inherited action table, although its narrowed angular primary can still improve. The timer says it resets on birth but does not say whether coherence recovery or O becoming empty resets it; cumulative and consecutive low-coherence rules differ.

**Fix:** register the exact estimator, carrier treatment, sample inclusion after births/deaths, history warm-up and every reset condition. State whether O is intentionally a single oscillator. If multiple outputs are intended, choose a demand rule that can actually request them from a singleton state. Keep the angular-only perceive scope explicit and report its distance error; do not present a singleton's C = 1 as accurate distance perception.

### 6. R2-6 — HIGH: reciprocal donor replay breaks the 127-df independent-episode t bound

**Evidence:** R6:131 specifies a paired-t lower bound over 128 episodes with 127 df. R6:142 maps each episode to the episode 64 positions away in the same panel, yielding 64 reciprocal pairs. Both differences in a pair depend on the same two world streams.

An on-paper counterexample is an ideal direction tracker. Let its own-input angular error be zero, and let its donor-input error be the mean absolute wrapped difference between the two truth-angle streams. Because that distance is symmetric, the two reciprocal episodes have **identical** donor differences. There are 64 independent pair units, not 128 independent differences. The stated calculation can therefore understate uncertainty. The error does not disappear because each episode is separately scored or because training seeds are independent.

The map is also ambiguous for literal world episode ids 512–639: `(e + 64) mod 128` returns 0–127, contrary to the fresh-panel statement. `rev6_donor` is listed but not connected to the map. G0 refers to an upper bound even though the registered estimator describes only a lower bound.

**Fix:** choose one contract before results: use unique donors from a disjoint, explicitly seeded donor panel, or treat reciprocal pairs as clusters and register pair-mean inference (64 units, with the corresponding df and assumptions). Write the mapping using local indices and actual world ids, freeze donor permutations/carrier alignment, and state all entropy keys. Give the lower/upper-bound formulas, negative/zero/positive constant dispositions and nonfinite handling. Retain the explicit distinction between conditional episode uncertainty and independent training-seed development counts. The conjunction of comparators for one primary task does not itself require Bonferroni; the donor dependence is the issue here.

### 7. R2-7 — HIGH: sham minus path lesion is not an intact-response decrement

**Evidence:** R6:143–149 requires a positive lower bound for `sham score − path-lesion score`. The proposed sham is itself a destructive intervention into arbitrary non-output receivers. It can remove irrelevant nodes, remove a real bottleneck, or improve selection by suppressing interference. There is no intact-versus-path-lesion decrement requirement or constraint that the sham preserve intact performance. It is possible for sham > path while path ≥ intact, satisfying this contrast without the claimed decrement from intact.

The mask and unweighted role readout do make the output-incoming-coupling cut a sensible terminal channel lesion **if plasticity is frozen and roles are preserved**. With that cut, output geometry cannot influence its readout weight, so the earlier input→motion→weighted-readout bypass is closed. That sound property does not turn destructive random receiver cuts into a neutral sham or establish which internal bridge carried the response.

**Fix:** require a registered positive bound for `intact − path lesion`. Use an inert sham that preserves the relevant channel, or additionally require a preregistered sham-versus-intact preservation tolerance and retain destructive receiver lesions as a separate control. Define without-replacement sampling, when receiver ids are selected, whether selection is held for the episode, insufficient-candidate behavior and lesion application at every RK4 stage. Call this an output-channel necessity test; add targeted internal frontier/bridge lesions only if the claim names those bridges as the mediator.

### 8. R2-8 — MEDIUM: G2 can show arbitrary-item routing without selection

**Evidence:** R6:6 requires the grown medium to “select among” its inputs, and R6:152 describes a fixed physical-site relay as near chance because sites are permuted. Permutation prevents persistent item identity, but does not make the relayed item independent of the correct answer: sometimes it is the nearest enemy.

At the initial perceive observation, enemy bearings are independent uniform angles and nearest distance chooses the truth (world source: `world/world.cpp:75–108, 115–118, 164–167`). The nearest enemy maps to any one physical site with probability 1/8. For an ideal fixed-site relay with a default when that site is inactive, its expected initial angular error is `(7/8)·(π/2) = 7π/16`, below a random/default angle's π/2, without selecting by salience. This is an initial-frame analytic example, **not** a measured 16-second panel score. A donor replay and a channel lesion can remove that incidental correct-item information too.

Thus beating default/random/donor and depending on coupling is consistent with a relay that never chooses the strongest input. The broader scope withdrawal is welcome; the remaining selection language exceeds the endpoint.

**Fix:** either call G2 basic input-dependent angular response/routing, with selection explicitly unqualified, or require a positive registered comparison against a properly defined fixed-site/arbitrary-item relay under the same exposure and decoder. Specify inactive-site behavior and evaluate the relay instead of assuming its full-episode score. The strongest-site oracle remains a direction-selection reference, not evidence of accurate perceive distance decoding or learned transformation.

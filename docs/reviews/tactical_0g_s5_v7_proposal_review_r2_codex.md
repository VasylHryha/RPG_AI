## Findings and concrete fixes

### R2-1 — HIGH: lowering the means does not make the proposed planning distribution conservative

Proposal §4, lines 79–88. The regular validation clusters have win fractions 0 in 2 clusters, 0.5 in 4, and 1 in 14: mean 0.8 and sample SD 0.3402785. The natural contraction about 0.5 to mean 0.70 has factor 2/3. It transforms their support to {1/6, 1/2, 5/6}, and reduces sample SD to 0.2268523, or variance by 4/9. These artificial bounded observations cannot occur as averages of two binary fight wins.

The novice case is decisive: all 20 development clusters have win fraction 1. Contracting them to mean 0.90 produces a constant 0.90 with variance zero. Any genuine {0, 1/2, 1}-valued distribution with mean 0.90 has variance at least **0.04** (attained by probabilities 0.2 at 0.5 and 0.8 at 1). Thus the proposed novice law removes loss uncertainty rather than conservatively representing it. An inferential bound can remain valid on artificial bounded data while its simulated power fails to describe the proposed experiment.

Resampling paired development rows does retain dependence under a declared transformation, but it retains only the observed support. It cannot create unseen adverse outcomes or reliability failures. Lower planning means (+2 instead of +4.675, 0.70 instead of 0.80) do not establish an uncertainty allowance for a selected 20-cluster validation or prove pessimism for the unknown population. This is a defect in the power justification, not a claim that every simulation of that law must overestimate power.

The validation split is fresh and did not select ordinal 161. Consequently, direct tuning winner's curse is not the correct explanation for its 32/40. Conditional optimism remains because drafting proceeds after the successful W4 gate and an outcome-informed design sequence. The draft acknowledges this, but saying contraction “handles” it asserts more than the evidence establishes. The existing v7c validation satisfies DESIGN §6's literal separate-development-validation requirement; **another fight panel is not automatically required**.

**Fix:** replace the contraction as the decisive sizing law with an explicitly specified, achievable joint cluster model or a declared set of adverse scenarios covering orientation dependence, score spread, rare losses and failure coding. Choose n against the least favorable declared scenario, and label power as conditional on those assumptions. Any shifted/scaled score law must remain inside the registered range and respect the declared win/score relationship if presented as a physical outcome model. Alternatively, retain the transformed empirical law only as a labelled sensitivity calculation and obtain an owner-approved conservative sizing basis. A prospectively fixed, separately authorized sizing split can help assess uncertainty, but do not require it by default or keep sampling development splits until one passes. Correct the self-audit's claim that finding 5 is fully fixed.

### R2-2 — MEDIUM before sizing/sealing: the exact betting decision and power calculation remain undefined

Proposal §4, lines 59–89. **The statistical family is valid:** bounded i.i.d. cluster means meet its assumptions; score normalization (S+50)/100 is correct. The paper's Theorem 3 supplies a weighted hedge and predictable clipped bets; Remark 3 gives a fixed-n construction. Neither requires observed variance to be positive. Taking the lower endpoint of a valid 99.5% two-sided set is conservative for a one-sided 99.5% bound. A positive-capital construction can instead use the full one-sided allocation. These choices have different power. [Waudby-Smith and Ramdas, primary paper, Theorem 3 and Remark 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09686).

For clarity, the following is my mathematical check, not a selected replacement implementation. For X_i in [0,1], choose predictable nonnegative bets λ_i(m)<1/m and define K_n(m)=∏[1+λ_i(m)(X_i−m)]. Under a conditional mean ≤m, its conditional expected multiplier is ≤1; nonnegative capital therefore yields a level-alpha test at threshold 1/alpha. The paper's hedge uses **max{h K⁺, (1−h) K⁻}**, not an unweighted maximum of two full-budget processes. Here h denotes the hedge weight, distinct from controller theta*. Setting h=1 gives the positive construction. Using a valid two-sided hedge and retaining its lower endpoint is also acceptable; no extra component split is required merely because that bound is conservative.

**All-win boundary check:** an illustrative choice λ_i(m)=c/m, 0<c<1, gives K_n(m)=[1+c(1−m)/m]^n when every X_i=1. Its lower boundary is c/[alpha^(−1/n)−1+c], strictly below 1 for finite n. Constant observations therefore do not justify collapsing the bound to 1. Correct endpoint limits and initialization avoid division by zero; no outcome-selected fallback is needed. This witness establishes feasibility, not the power of an unspecified recommended bet sequence.

The proposal promises to pin an algorithm later. That is a reasonable proposal-to-specification division, but the sizing receipt cannot precede selecting the algorithm it simulates. Freeze the bet formula, prior mean/variance initialization, truncation, hedge weight, fixed-time versus confidence-sequence construction, final-time versus running-intersection inversion, cluster processing order, log-capital arithmetic, conservative numerical enclosure and [0,1] boundary treatment. Define strict threshold comparisons and analysis-error handling. A bet may depend on earlier observations, not the current or later judged outcome. Freeze this before power planning, with the same decision code used for planning and eventual analysis.

The simulation also needs the input hash and exact pair extraction, transformation equations, resampling weights, RNG/implementation version, actual fixed seed, ordering, replication count and per-head selection/stop rule. Uniformly resampling the 20 regular development pairs gives score SD **7.9219237** for the empirical probability law; **8.1277222** is their sample SD with denominator 19. A shift alone cannot make these identical. Pin which convention and any explicit rescaling are intended rather than claiming both.

At 20,000 simulated panels, estimated power near 0.90 has Monte Carlo standard error about **0.00212**. A point estimate crossing 0.90 is not a guarantee that underlying simulated-law power is at least 0.90. Either explicitly call the criterion estimated power, or use a predeclared lower Monte Carlo confidence bound with an allocation covering the searched candidates and heads. Do not change seeds or simulation assumptions to obtain a desired n.

**60 is not justified.** It is correctly no longer fixed. As a scale check only, a known-SD normal score test at mean +2, SD 8.13 and one-sided alpha 0.005 has marginal score power about **0.251 at n=60** and **0.817 at n=200**, and needs approximately **246** clusters for 0.90 marginal power. Endpoint joint power cannot exceed its score component's power. These are illustrative normal calculations, **not exact betting power, a registered result or proof that the cap must fail**. They show why the exact receipt is indispensable and why the proposed 200 cap may trigger the owner stop.

**Fix:** make the algorithm and repaired alternatives concrete, then publish the reproducible per-head joint-power table and selected n before any judging entropy. Apply the owner stop if no candidate qualifies. The 200 cap is an explicit proposed replacement of DESIGN §10's older 2,000-cluster trade-off boundary, not an inherited owner-approved number. Seal the owner's agreement to n, the cap and the S conjunct. Choosing fixed n using development evidence does not itself inflate judging alpha; the unresolved problem is the power justification.
